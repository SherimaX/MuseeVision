ROOM 01 · SHEET 3 OF 4 · DETAILS

Details

Classical bones throughout: a coffered barrel vault on transverse arches, travertine piers, a plaster cornice, silk walls. The light is hidden in the architecture: a glass lantern in each bay's crown, a cove behind the cornice that washes the vault, and a concealed spot for every painting high in the vault. Nothing is fixed beside the art. The paintings wear carved gilt frames, a glass stele carries the first of them, and the floor holds flush viewing stones.

TRANSVERSE SECTION THROUGH BAY 2 · LOOKING WEST
THE NYMPHÉAS OVAL · SECTION · LOOKING WEST
Lower and softer than the bays: lime-white plaster walls 5 m high, then a deep cove up to a luminous velarium that glows with the daylight. The four panels hang 0.7 m above the floor, 2 m tall and unframed, so the water lilies sit at the level of your gaze. At the centre, a pond of living water lilies, ringed by four curved travertine benches.

THE VIEWING STONE
Set flush into the floor wherever a curator would want you to stand: eighteen of them, from Bay 1 to the far side of the pond.
THE WALL LABEL
Look at a work for a moment and its label fades in: artist, title, date, medium, collection and a line of notes on a travertine card. Look away and it fades.
