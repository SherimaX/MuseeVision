MODERN · ALIVE · EAST OF THE ROTUNDA

Hall of Light

A glass hall 9 metres wide and 28 long on its axis, from the Rotunda's drum to the Atrium's stone base, between two gardens. Glass walls 5 m high stand on a travertine plinth under a shallow vault of pearl-white steel and glass. Twenty photographs, 1840 to 1914, stand along the glass with the garden behind them.

Four stereo stones

Round drums of honed travertine, 0.7 m across and 1 m high, 2.4 m off the axis, north and south in turn. A bronze reader at 1.32 m shows each card whole and centred: both views, side by side.

Two gardens, in season

North of the glass, eight silver birches stand in a meadow of scabious, mallow, helenium and buttercups. South, ten cherries and apples stand in grass over spring bulbs. Leaves, blossom, fruit and flowers follow the season, and all of it moves in the wind.

Prints on bronze stems

Two prints to each 3 m bay, one on each wall, on pale mounts 1.0 × 0.9 m (1.15 to 2.05 m high) on two bronze stems just inside the glass, each in a thin dark oak frame. In bays 9 and 10 four autochromes are set into the glass between bronze glazing bars, on white mats of the same size. From behind you see a backing board.

Light, by day and night

By day only the sun and the sky light the hall, through the glass. At night 20 hidden uplights on the eaves wash the pearl vault and 20 path lights low in the plinth light the floor, all at 3800 K; they fade out as day comes. Each photograph has its own accent spot, 70 lux at 4000 K.
