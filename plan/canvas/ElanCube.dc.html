SECTION II · ÉLAN · LEVEL −1 · EARTH

The Cube

Under the round Atrium lies a cube, 28 m each way: the Sphere’s twin, mirrored in the Atrium floor. Its six faces are light-field panels that fill the room with light in three dimensions, so the walls seem to vanish. The glass car comes down through the ground and stops at the exact centre, 22 m below the Atrium floor, as far down as the Sphere’s centre is up. It never lands: the whole journey, a mountain or the sea, is lived from the car.

HOW THE ROOM MAKES LIGHT

Near: in the room

Rays from many panels cross at one point in mid-air, so a real point of light hangs there. Everyone in the car sees it from their own side, without glasses.

Far: beyond the walls

Rays can also leave a panel as if they came from far behind it. Then the wall is a window, and a peak 5 km away looks 5 km away.

At the centre

Every face is 14 m away, above and below too, so the room vanishes evenly all round. Nothing solid is in view but the car’s floor, its rail and the gilt mast.

THE TECHNOLOGY · FEASIBLE, NOT YET BUILT

Light-field panels

Every face is tiled with 0.7 m panels, 40 by 40, 9,600 in all, and each pixel sends different light in different directions. Such panels exist as small modules today (Light Field Lab’s SolidLight), but no one has built a room of them. The six faces come to 4,704 m², about a third of the Las Vegas Sphere’s inner screen: the size is not the problem, the pixel density and the computing are.

Ultrasound

Arrays of ultrasonic emitters sit behind the panels, which let sound through. They put each sound where its thing is, so the ice creaks beside you and a manta passes overhead, and they can press lightly on your hand. This works at hand size today (Ultraleap; the University of Sussex’s acoustic display, 2019), not yet in a room. In the headset it becomes spatial sound, and things that answer your hand.

The car at the centre

The car comes down a glass shaft through soil, clay and chalk. The iris in the Cube’s ceiling opens, and the car slides down a gilt mast hung from it, the Sphere’s mast upside down, until your eyes are at the centre; the iris shuts behind it, round the mast. The glass clears to a floor and a rail. Afterwards the car rises straight through the Atrium to the Sphere: the earth first, then the sky.
