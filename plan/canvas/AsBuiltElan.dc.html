AS BUILT · FUTURE · ÉLAN

The Atrium: twelve ribs and an iris

The Atrium's stone pilasters have gone. In their place, twelve pearl ribs rise from the floor, arch under the Sphere and meet at the ring round the glass car's way. They began as bones after Calatrava's Oculus, but read as roots under a bulb, so they are now drawn as structure: one constant section from floor to ring.

THE GLASS

Glass you can see, everywhere

The car's panes follow real glass. Face on they almost vanish. Towards the edges they mirror the room and close up, and a faint haze of use lies on them. Every pane in the museum is now this glass. The neck between the floors is pearl, lit at daylight white.

THE IRIS

Seven petals, not teeth

Seven curved blades, 1.25 m wide, each a 110° arc of a circle with round ends, turn on pins round the opening. They sweep in together like a camera's iris: 52° of swing, eased, over four slow seconds. Closed, they are the floor you stand on in the Sphere and the ceiling over the Atrium.

WHERE THEY ARE

Three on the ride

One seals the Atrium's ceiling, hidden in the pearl collar when open. One closes the Sphere's floor opening at the top of the neck, flush with its bronze ring. One is the platform's own floor in the Sphere, lined in pearl. The seats are 96-sided rings, so the blades close on a true circle.
