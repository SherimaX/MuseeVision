One building, three eras

A museum you step into. Each room keeps its classical proportions while taking one more step, eastward, toward modern light and future glass.

The floor keeps the hour

A 14 m bronze sundial in the tradition of Rome’s meridian lines. Motto: HORAS NON NUMERO NISI SERENAS.

The one touch of future

The sun keeps your local time on an even path, 15° an hour in every season, so the point always lands on the hour.

A lattice in the eye

A regular steel-and-glass grid in the spirit of the British Museum’s Great Court. On a rainy day you hear the rain on the glass.

How the museum is lit

By day

Daylight comes through the laylights and skylights, glowing with the sky. The Salon and Sculpture Hall are roofed over their laylights, so no sun falls on a painting.

The sun alone

The Rotunda has no lamps: the sun through its eye draws the lattice and the point of shadow on the clock. The Hall of Light is glass, and full of sun.

A light for every work

Each painting has its own warm light, about 3000 K. Works on paper and photographs get less light, sculptures more.

At night

The laylights dim and pools of light on the works carry the rooms. The sky and the sun clock follow the real time where you are.
