# Chenghuai 澄懷 · image prompts (set 2)

The north wing of Musée Vision: a three-court Beijing house (三进四合院), 澄懷, with the garden 臥遊 beside it. Source: the Chenghuai design canvas (https://claude.ai/artifact/MMmko4Lwg1TGgb8SYzmZoT), especially its Names, Rooms and Materials boards.

**How to use.** One prompt per view, in visit order. Attach the matching `NN-chenghuai-…-composition.png` (the plan with the camera and view cone in red, plus the elevation of what you are looking at when there is one), then paste the view prompt followed by the shared block.

**What changed from set 1.** Every inscription now uses the new names, spelled out character by character, including the left-to-right order in which the characters should appear. Beam painting is restrained, the way a scholar-official's house would be. Rooms and courts are held to their true, small sizes. Specific fixes:
- 10: the door of the 广亮大门 is set deep; a short porch replaces the plaza; no door studs.
- 13: the 垂花门 has a front ridged roof and a rounded rear roof.
- 14: the side halls have rounded roofs; the 正房 has a higher plinth and a couplet.
- 15: Fan Kuan is the tallest and the three landscapes are described.
- 16: the room is narrow and the light diffuse.
- 19: no city; the Rotunda is close and correct.
- 21: the 正房's back wall faces the camera, and the gate and Rotunda share one line.

---

**Shared block (paste after every prompt):**

> Photorealistic architectural photograph, 3:2 landscape, straight verticals, natural light, real material textures with slight honest weathering. The place is a real, newly built Qing-dynasty Beijing courtyard house of a scholar-official, made by traditional craftsmen and used as a museum; it is a house, not a palace or a temple, and its rooms and courts are small and intimate at the sizes given. Materials: grey reduction-fired brick with fine rubbed joints; unglazed grey clay tiles; columns and door leaves in vermilion lacquer; plinths, steps and column bases in pale blue-grey Fangshan stone; floors of large square grey clay tiles; lattice doors and windows glazed with translucent mulberry paper, so daylight indoors is a soft even glow, never sharp gridded sun patterns. Painted decoration is restrained: most beam surfaces are plain dark green or slate blue, painted bands only at the beam ends, and at mid-span of each bay a single white semicircular panel with a small ink landscape; no dragons, phoenixes, dense patterns or heavy gilding. Garden buildings are not painted at all: chestnut-brown lacquered timber, white lime-rendered walls with dark tile copings. Museum fittings are modern and discreet: low-iron anti-reflective glass cases on unpainted golden phoebe-wood plinths, no visible lamps, ducts, labels or signs. Any Chinese inscription appears exactly as specified, in traditional characters, and nowhere else: no other text, captions, logos or UI. No door studs (门钉), no red lanterns, no plastic, no neon, no modern buildings, towers, city skyline or power lines anywhere in view. Artworks are faithful reproductions of the named works at their true size. At most one or two visitors in plain modern clothing, for scale.

## 10 · Arrival: 广亮大门 from the Rotunda

Composition reference: `10-chenghuai-arrival-composition.png` · eye level 1.6 m · 24 mm

Standing inside the Rotunda of Musée Vision, just behind its north door, looking north through the tall arched doorway of pale marble (4 m wide, in cool shadow) along a short open-air stone passage, 3.8 m wide and 5 m long, between two low grey-brick walls about 1.2 m high. At its end, 7 m from the camera, is the gate of the house: a guangliang gate (广亮大门) of official rank. It is one bay, 3.6 m wide, on a 0.9 m stone platform with five steps. Its front is open: two red eave columns with carved brackets, and a painted beam with one white semicircular landscape panel. The pair of vermilion door leaves stands deep inside, about 2.5 m back, at the line of the ridge columns, so the front half of the gate is a shaded porch. Above the leaves are four hexagonal door pins; beside the threshold, a pair of box-shaped carved stone door piers (门墩). The plain vermilion leaves carry a carved door couplet in gold, each line reading top to bottom: 芝蘭君子性 on the right leaf, 松柏古人心 on the left leaf. The gate's grey tile roof has small upturned ridge ends and a ridge about 6.5 m high. To the left, above the low wall, is the plain grey-brick back wall of the house's front row with its lower tile roof. To the right is a grey-brick garden wall 3.6 m high with one round lattice window; over it rise bamboo, a red maple, a dark lacebark pine, and the upturned roof of a small hexagonal pavilion on a rockery. Late-morning sun from behind the camera falls on the gate, and the deep door recess stays in soft shadow.

## 11 · 影壁 · 澄懷觀道

Composition reference: `11-chenghuai-yingbi-composition.png` · eye level 1.6 m · 24 mm

Just through the gate, standing between the open vermilion leaves in the dim gate passage and looking north into a small sunlit entry yard, only 3.6 m wide and 5.6 m deep. Straight ahead, 5 m away, a screen wall (影壁) is built against the yard's north wall. It is finely rubbed grey brick with no visible joints, a carved-brick frame with small corner flowers, and a stepped brick cornice under a small tile roof. At its centre is a square carved-brick panel about 1.8 m across, bearing four characters in relief arranged in two vertical columns: the right column reads 澄懷 from top to bottom, the left column reads 觀道 from top to bottom. To the left (west), in a plain brick partition, is a doorway of four green-lacquered screen leaves (屏门), standing open onto the long front court. Grey brick paving. Quiet and compressed: the first turn of the visit.

## 12 · 倒座房 · 臨池

Composition reference: `12-chenghuai-linchi-composition.png` · eye level 1.6 m · 24 mm

Inside the front row of the house (倒座房), a hall 17.9 m long, 4.8 m wide and about 3.6 m to the beams, open to fir rafters and red-pine beams. Standing at its west end and looking east down its length. On the right (south), a solid lime-plastered wall. Along it runs one continuous slanted display case, 15 m long at table height, with an unpainted golden phoebe-wood base and a low-iron glass lid. Five Chinese calligraphy handscrolls lie open in it, end to end, at their true sizes:
- nearest, the *Orchid Pavilion Preface* in Feng Chengsu's Tang copy (24.5 × 69.9 cm, small running script);
- then Yan Zhenqing's *Draft Eulogy for a Nephew* (28 × 75 cm, a draft full of crossings-out);
- Huai Su's *Autobiography* in wild cursive running 7.5 m;
- Su Shi's *Cold Food Observance* (34 × 200 cm);
- Mi Fu's *Poems on Sichuan Silk* (28 × 271 cm), on silk with faint ruled columns.

On the left (north), a row of lattice windows glazed with mulberry paper gives soft, even north light; no sun enters. At the far east end, a Ming hardwood painting table with an inkstone, brushes in a bamboo pot and a sheet of paper. Above it, on the east wall, hangs a small black lacquer plaque with two gilt characters, 臨池, written right to left, so the characters appear left to right as 池 臨. Floor of 45 cm square clay tiles with hairline joints. One visitor bends over the case. Hushed and dim, a warm paper glow.

## 13 · 垂花门 · 齋莊中正

Composition reference: `13-chenghuai-chuihuamen-composition.png` · eye level 1.6 m · 24 mm

In the narrow front court (5.6 m deep, grey-brick paved), looking north at the festooned gate (垂花门) in the middle of a cross wall. The gate stands on a 0.45 m stone platform with two steps. Its front eaves rest on two short hanging posts that stop in mid-air, each ending in a carved gilt lotus bud; between them runs an openwork carved frieze. The beams are painted restrainedly, with one white semicircular landscape panel. It has red columns and two roofs, one behind the other (一殿一卷): a steep front roof with a ridge and overhanging gables, and behind it a lower, rounded roof without a ridge. Through its open red leaves you see four closed green-lacquered screen leaves, each with a red roundel bearing one gold character. They read right to left as 齋 莊 中 正, so from left to right the leaves show 正, 中, 莊, 齋. On both sides the cross wall is white lime plaster above a grey-brick base, with a fan-shaped lattice window in each half. Clear midday light; the pink blossom of a crabapple shows above the wall.

## 14 · 内院 · 澄懷堂

Composition reference: `14-chenghuai-inner-court-composition.png` · eye level 1.6 m · 24 mm

Standing in the inner court just inside the festooned gate, looking north. The court is only 12 m wide and 15 m deep, paved in square grey bricks, and crossed by raised stone paths that meet at a glazed ceramic fish bowl. At the far end stands the main hall (正房) on a 0.75 m stone plinth with three steps. It is three bays wide and clearly taller than everything around it. It has a deep veranda, 1.3 m, carried on red columns 3.9 m tall. The centre bay has lattice doors; the side bays have lattice windows over grey-brick sills; all are glazed with glowing mulberry paper. Over the centre bay hangs a black lacquer plaque with three gilt characters, 澄懷堂, written right to left, so they appear left to right as 堂 懷 澄. On the two centre veranda columns hang a pair of long black boards with gilt characters, each reading top to bottom: 行到水窮處 on the right column, 坐看雲起時 on the left. The hall has a grey barrel-tile roof with small upturned ridge ends, about 8 m high. Lower side rooms (耳房) step back on either side. To the left and right are the west and east side halls (厢房) on lower plinths, with rounded, ridgeless roofs (卷棚), joined to the gate and the hall by covered galleries (抄手游廊) with red posts and low seat rails that wrap the corners of the court. Two crabapple trees in pink blossom stand in the north beds and two lilacs in the south beds, with pomegranates in wooden tubs flanking the hall steps. Spring, late-morning sun.

## 15 · 正房 · 澄懷堂, inside

Composition reference: `15-chenghuai-chenghuai-tang-composition.png` · eye level 1.6 m · 24 mm

Inside the main hall, 10 m wide and 6 m deep, open to painted beams and rafters, standing in the centre doorway and looking north at the back wall, 6 m away. Three glass cases are set into the lime-plastered wall, one per bay, each on a golden phoebe-wood plinth. Each holds one monumental Northern Song hanging scroll in its full pale silk mount:
- **Centre, and the tallest of the three, as the 中堂:** Fan Kuan's *Travellers among Mountains and Streams*, painting 206 × 103 cm, mount 3.2 m tall. A single immense rock mountain fills the upper two-thirds, with a thread of waterfall at its right. Below, in mist, is a band of trees and boulders with a tiny mule train on the lower right. It hangs above a Ming hardwood altar table with upturned ends.
- **West bay (left):** Guo Xi's *Early Spring*, painting 158 × 108 cm, shorter than Fan Kuan. Mountains in curling, cloud-like forms rise through spring mist, with gnarled trees in the foreground.
- **East bay (right):** Li Tang's *Wind in Pines among a Myriad Valleys*, painting 189 × 140 cm, the widest but still lower than Fan Kuan. It shows a tall rock peak behind dense dark pines and a waterfall.

Between the bays, delicate carved openwork screens (花罩) of unpainted golden phoebe wood hang from the beams, open, so all three scrolls are seen together. The columns are red. The beams are plain dark green with painted ends and one white landscape panel per bay. The floor is 54 cm clay tiles, oiled dark and faintly gleaming. The only daylight comes softly through the paper-glazed lattice behind the camera, and the cases are lit invisibly. It is dim, even and still. One visitor stands before Fan Kuan.

## 16 · 西厢房 · 天青

Composition reference: `16-chenghuai-tianqing-composition.png` · eye level 1.6 m · 20 mm

Inside the west side hall. It is a narrow room: 9.4 m long but only 3.9 m deep, with red columns, lime-white walls and open rafters. Standing in its court door and looking west at the back wall, only 3.5 m away; the room runs on out of the frame to the left and right. Directly ahead, alone in a small square glass case on a phoebe-wood pedestal 1 m high, is the Ru ware narcissus basin: 23 cm across, oval and shallow, with a pale sky-blue celadon glaze and a fine crackle. Behind it, along the back wall, a long glass wall case on a wooden plinth holds ceramics at true size:
- to the left, a Tang sancai-glazed horse about 70 cm tall, and a white Ding bowl;
- to the right, a crackled grey Guan vase, a black Jian "hare's fur" tea bowl and a green Longquan celadon mallet vase;
- a Jun flowerpot with purple splashes in the centre, partly hidden behind the Ru case.

Morning light comes through the paper-glazed lattice behind the camera as a soft, bright, even glow that makes the glazes shine. There are no sun stripes. Clear and luminous.

## 17 · 后罩房 · 舒卷

Composition reference: `17-chenghuai-shujuan-composition.png` · eye level 1.6 m · 24 mm

The rear row of the house (后罩房), a long, low hall 22 m long and 4.7 m wide, seen from its east end looking west down its length. Along the right (north) wall runs a long slanted display case with a phoebe-wood base and a glass lid, holding two Northern Song handscrolls unrolled in full at their true size. Nearest is Zhang Zeduan's *Along the River during the Qingming Festival* (25 cm × 5.3 m), in brown ink and pale colour, with a crowded arched bridge. Beyond it, receding down the room, is Wang Ximeng's *A Thousand Li of Rivers and Mountains* (51.5 cm × 11.9 m), in brilliant mineral blue and green. On the left (south), a continuous row of lattice doors and windows glazed in mulberry paper glows warm with afternoon light; outside, bamboo blinds are half-lowered. Above the far west doorway hangs a small black plaque with gilt characters, 舒卷, written right to left, so they appear left to right as 卷 舒. Open fir rafters, restrained painted beams, red columns, clay-tile floor. Two visitors walk slowly beside the *Thousand Li*.

## 18 · 月洞门 · 臥遊

Composition reference: `18-chenghuai-woyou-composition.png` · eye level 1.6 m · 24 mm

In the narrow rear court of the house (only 4.2 m deep), between two old jujube trees, looking east at a circular moon gate 2.4 m across, cut through the plain grey-brick east wall of the house. Through the circle the materials change. The garden beyond is Suzhou in manner: white lime-rendered walls under dark tile copings, chestnut-brown timber, and a pebble-mosaic path. About 3 m inside the gate, framed exactly by the circle, stands a single tall Taihu limestone rock, about 3 m high, pale grey and pierced with holes, rising from a low bed of moss. Two characters, 石兄, are cut vertically into its face (石 above 兄) and filled with faded red. Behind it are bamboo, the edge of a lotus pond, and the chestnut posts of a covered gallery. Above the moon gate, on the court side, is a small unpainted wooden plaque carved with two characters, 臥遊, written right to left, so they appear left to right as 遊 臥. Soft afternoon light, with the garden brighter than the court.

## 19 · 亭 · 見山

Composition reference: `19-chenghuai-jianshan-composition.png` · eye level about 4.5 m (on the rockery) · 28 mm

From inside a small hexagonal pavilion on top of a grey limestone rockery at the south end of the garden, looking south-west. The pavilion has plain chestnut-brown lacquered posts, a low seat rail and no painted decoration. In the foreground, the rockery falls away through bamboo and a red maple to the garden's white wall. Just beyond the wall, 10 to 15 m away, the grey tile roofs of the courtyard house step down: the long front row, and the gate roof with its small upturned ridge ends. Beyond them, only 30 m from the camera and large in the frame, rises the Rotunda of Musée Vision. It is a pale marble drum 22 m across and 10 m high, with slender pilasters, crowned by a low coffered stone dome. At the dome's top sits a round eye 5 m across, covered by a small triangulated steel-and-glass lattice. Nothing else is on the horizon: only trees and sky, no city. Late-afternoon light and long shadows across the roofs. The pavilion's name, 見山, "seeing the mountain", comes from 悠然見南山, and this dome to the south is the mountain it means.

## 20 · 花厅 · 林泉

Composition reference: `20-chenghuai-linquan-composition.png` · eye level 1.6 m · 24 mm

Inside the flower hall of the garden, a small hall 7.3 m wide and 3.6 m deep, of chestnut-brown lacquered posts and lattice with white walls. Standing at its north side and looking south. All its south doors are folded open onto a lotus pond crossed by a zigzag stone bridge, with a willow and a small water pavilion beyond. Above the open doors, inside the hall, hangs a plain wooden plaque with two green-filled characters, 林泉, written right to left, so they appear left to right as 泉 林. In the middle of the room stands a Ming huanghuali painting table with a hanging scroll half-unrolled on it, held flat by two bronze weights. On the table are a Duan inkstone, brushes in a bamboo brush pot, a small dark Lingbi scholar's rock on a carved stand, and a single plum branch in a celadon vase. A pair of Ming yoke-back armchairs waits at the table. There is nothing else in the room: no side tables, no potted plants. Square grey floor tiles. It is the room where friends once unrolled paintings together, empty for a moment: calm and domestic.

## 21 · Aerial from the north-east

Composition reference: `21-chenghuai-aerial-composition.png` · camera about 45 m up · 35 mm

An aerial view from the north-east, about 45 m up, looking south-west over the whole wing.
- **The garden:** nearest the camera, on the left, is a long, narrow Suzhou-style garden, 15 × 45 m, inside white walls. It has a flower hall at its north end on a lotus pond, a zigzag bridge, a water pavilion against the east wall, a grey rockery with a small hexagonal pavilion at its south end, and a covered gallery along its west side, against the house.
- **The house:** to the garden's right is the three-court Beijing house, 23 × 45 m, with grey tile roofs in strict symmetry. Nearest is the long rear row, then a narrow rear court with two trees. Then comes the main hall, whose plain rear wall with small high windows faces the camera; its lattice front and veranda face south, away from us, onto a square inner court with four flowering trees and a fish bowl. Galleries wrap the court's corners. Then come the festooned gate in a cross wall, a long front court, and the front row.
- **The gate:** at the south-east corner of the house, where house and garden meet, sits the red gate. A short walled passage runs straight south from it into the Rotunda.
- **The Rotunda:** a pale marble drum with a low coffered dome and a small glass lattice eye. It lies on the gate's line, south-east of the house's centre, not on the house's own axis.
- **Beyond:** to the right (west) of the Rotunda runs the long, stone, skylit Salon; to the left (east), a long glass hall among gardens.

Everything else is trees; no roads, no city. Soft morning light, clear air, a real place.
