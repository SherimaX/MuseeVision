PROGRAMME · ROOM BY ROOM

Rooms made for the works

隨物賦形

Every room was chosen for what it holds: the light it gets, its height, the case it needs. Works marked [loan to select] are types to borrow, not yet named pieces. Dimensions are the works’ own.

01大门 · 影壁澄懷觀道
Chénghuái guān dào · the only door
gate hall 3.6 × 5.5 m · entry yard 3.6 × 5.6 m
open air

The wing’s one door, on the Rotunda axis: you leave by the gate you came in. The carved-brick 影壁 facing the gate carries 宗炳’s four characters, 澄懷觀道, and turns you west through a green 屏门.

  • 影壁心, carved brick: 澄懷觀道
    new commission, 1.8 × 1.8 m
  • Pair of box-type 门墩, 青白石
    part of the gate

03垂花门 · 游廊齋莊中正
the screen that stays shut · circulation, no art
galleries 1.3 m wide, about 21 m in all
open, covered

The green 屏门 (齋莊中正) stays shut except on festival days, as in a lived-in house. You go round by the 抄手游廊. Stairs, not ramps: the galleries step up twice between the side halls and the hall, as in the house they copy.

05正房澄懷堂
Chénghuái Táng · three Northern Song landscapes
10.0 × 6.1 m + 1.3 m veranda · 3 bays · open to the rafters
faces south · paper-glazed, 6 m deep · 50 lux · 50 % RH

The hall of the house, the heart of the wing: one monumental scroll per bay, each in a full-height case in the back wall. Fan Kuan hangs in the middle as the 中堂, over a Ming altar table. Open carved 花罩, not walls, divide the bays, so the three can be seen together from the door. The columns are raised to 3.9 m (the house rule of 0.8 × bay would give 3.1 m) so the full mounts fit, 3.2 m for Fan Kuan.

  • Guo Xi, Early Spring, 1072 · west bay
    158.3 × 108.1 cm · NPM Taipei
  • Fan Kuan, Travellers among Mountains and Streams · centre
    206.3 × 103.3 cm · NPM Taipei
  • Li Tang, Wind in Pines among a Myriad Valleys, 1124 · east bay
    188.7 × 139.8 cm · NPM Taipei
  • Ming altar table (翘头案)

10后罩房舒卷
Shūjuǎn, unrolling · two handscrolls, 1100–1113

The house’s old storeroom becomes its treasury. Two slanted cases, 20.6 m in all, run along the north wall. You enter at the east and walk west, reading right to left as the scrolls were meant to be read.
