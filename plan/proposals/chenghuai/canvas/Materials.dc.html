MATERIALS · HOW IT IS BUILT

Built as it would be in Beijing

營造

Real Qing house carpentry and masonry, graded by rank like the house itself: the finest brickwork, tiles and floors on the gate and the main hall, plainer work at the back. The museum’s systems are hidden inside that fabric: air through the hollow plinths, light through paper, climate in the cases.

THE MUSEUM INSIDE THE HOUSE · 正房 AT 40° N

Light

Silk and paper: 50 lux, shown in rotation. Ceramics: 200 lux. Paper-laminated glazing, deep eaves and bamboo blinds keep direct sun off every work.

Climate

Cases hold 50 ± 5 % RH, buffered from a drawer in each 楠木 plinth. Rooms are only tempered, by air rising from the hollow plinths, so no ducts cut the timber.

Clean cases

No oak, MDF or camphor inside a seal (they give off acids). Steel-framed cases with low-iron laminated glass; every lining is tested before use.

Fire and night

Water mist in the roof space; the bronze 太平缸 fire vats stay in the courts. Plank shutters (板门) close the lattice at night.
