THE SOUTH SCREEN · IRON AND GLASS ABOVE THE TAPESTRY
Twelve of the thirteen Story of Tristram and Isoude panels Morris, Marshall, Faulkner & Co. made in 1862 for Walter Dunlop’s Harden Grange, near Bingley; owned by Bradford Museums and shown at Cliffe Castle, Keighley. Each is nearly square, about 68 × 60.5 cm; here they are enlarged about two and a half times to 1.6 × 1.7 m and read left to right, top row first. The thirteenth, King Arthur and Sir Launcelot (Morris), a coda to the story, stays out.

THE PORCH FLOOR
Encaustic tiles in red, buff and black, in the manner of Pugin’s for the Palace of Westminster, inside a black border. Over the door to the court, cut in the stone: rejecting nothing, selecting nothing, scorning nothing (Ruskin, Modern Painters, 1843).
FURNITURE · OAK
Four table cases of William De Morgan’s lustre stand in the aisles on lines 3 and 5, low enough to see the paintings over. The lectern holds the Kelmscott Chaucer open; two benches sit off the axis in the nave.
