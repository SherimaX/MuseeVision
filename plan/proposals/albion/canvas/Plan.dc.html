ALBION · 02 · PLAN AND ROOF

The plan

Lines 1 to 7, six bays of six metres

The court is 28 by 36 metres inside: a nave of 12 between aisles of 8, on column lines every 6 metres. You walk south down the west aisle and back up the east; nothing stands on the axis from the Rotunda’s gilt sun.

ROOF PLAN
London’s weather lands on all three roofs.

Floors

Nave: encaustic tiles in red, buff and black, laid on the diagonal. Aisles: York stone flags, 1.2 m. Porch: encaustic tiles in a black border.

Walls

Buff stone banded with red every 1.2 m, as Ruskin asked in The Stones of Venice; a slate skirting 0.2 m.

Columns

Ten polished British stones, Ø 0.5 m, on moulded bases; carved capitals at 5.5–6.2 m; clustered iron shafts to 10 m.

Roof

Three pointed glass vaults on iron ribs every 6 m, lead gutters on the arcade lines, a lead roof over the porch.

Doors

Rotunda to porch 4.0 m, round-arched; porch to court 4.0 m, pointed. No other doors: the court is a dead end, like the Nymphéas oval.

Cuts

A on the axis, looking east. B through bay 5, looking south. C through bay 1, looking north. See the Sections sheet.
