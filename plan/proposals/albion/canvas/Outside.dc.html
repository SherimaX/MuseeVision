ALBION · 06 · OUTSIDE AND SITE

Outside

A museum court, not a church

From outside Albion is a long, low hall of banded stone under three glass roofs, with iron-and-glass ends. No crosses, pinnacles, spires or rose windows: it should read as a museum or a train shed, never as a chapel.

Rain

Rain runs down the glass and drums on the ribs; the court goes a stop darker and the band dims.

Fog

Fog fills the vaults above the iron springs at 10 m; the paintings stay clear below it.

Snow

Snow settles along the three ridges and the light below turns blue and even.

Sun

The Tristram and Isolde band throws its colours up the nave floor, moving with London’s sun.

WHAT MOVES

The Chinese Wing moves north

Albion takes the Rotunda’s south door, so the Chinese Wing would move to the empty Sculpture Hall site on the north, where its 21 m square fits between the Manet cabinet and the meadow. Proposed; not yet decided.

The walk bends

The gravel walk from the front to the Élan now bends round Albion’s south end. The south gable becomes a second face beside the classical portico, as the Natural History Museum stands beside the V&A.
